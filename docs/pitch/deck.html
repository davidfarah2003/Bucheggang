01 / The problem

Let the agent shop.
Keep the customer in control.

A shopping agent needs permission to act. A payment credential is too much permission.

02 / The boundary

A mandate the customer actually sees

Agent proposes rules→Wallet shows saved draft→Customer confirms

The agent receives an opaque mandate ID. It cannot confirm the policy for the customer.

03 / The decision

Every purchase checked against that mandate

Purchase facts→Policy + history checks→Approve / decline / ask

Merchant instructions are untrusted data. The Wallet shows the reason when a purchase needs customer attention.

04 / Measured offline

Public-attempt replay

45 offline attempts under recorded evaluation policies. 43/45 had an initial decision without step-up. Correctness and ordinary-purchase utility require reconciled labels.

05 / Live demonstration

Watch the permission and the purchase

Propose a draft through MCP. Confirm the saved policy in the Wallet. Show the simulator's actual decision, evidence and any customer question.

Present the observed live outcome. The offline example is not a selectable live purchase or a substitute for a live demonstration.

06 / The opportunity

Agentic commerce with a customer-held leash

One control layer for bounded delegation, explainable intervention and a trusted customer decision surface.

Next evidence: reconciled expected-decision labels, a completed human Wallet step-up and a repeated live run.
