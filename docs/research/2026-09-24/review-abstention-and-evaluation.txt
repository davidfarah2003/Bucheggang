## Corrections

**C1. Direct three-class output.** Training a supervised three-class model needs ternary labels, and none exist. A pretrained model (arm D, an LLM judge) can emit approve/decline/step_up at inference without task labels. Neither can be evaluated or calibrated without adjudicated labels for the target, and history supplies none.

**C2. Conformal-set mapping (rank 4).**
- Marginal coverage P(Y ∈ C(X)) ≥ 1-α bounds the joint event P(approve and violating) ≤ α, because that event is a miscoverage.
- Given approval, it only yields P(violating | approve) ≤ α / P(approve). With α = 0.05 and a 25% approval share, that bound is 0.20.
- Empty sets must map to step_up.
- Conditional risk needs a selective method (SGR, LTT, SCRC-T, Trust or Escalate). Each of these needs labelled calibration data exchangeable with the target.

**C3. CRC** (arXiv 2208.02814 v4, 2025-06-13).
- The numbering is Theorem 2.1, Theorem 2.2 and Proposition 4.2.
- Theorem 2.1 requires exchangeability, non-increasing right-continuous losses bounded by B, and L_i(λmax) ≤ α.
- For a false-approval loss, all-step_up satisfies the risk constraint with loss 0. The guarantee is therefore always attainable by asking about everything, and it says nothing about friction.
- Theorem 2.2's lower bound needs i.i.d. data and no jumps.
- Proposition 4.2 sums TV(Z_i, Z_{n+1}) over all n calibration points. Under a fixed shift it grows with n, so it is vacuous here.

**C4. Yao 2010.** The threshold formulas (eq. 8) are correctly quoted. They require:
- the loss ordering (c0): λPP ≤ λBP < λNP and λNN ≤ λBN < λPN;
- condition (c1) for α > β;
- a calibrated Pr(compliant) on the target.

"Ask me when uncertain" gives only an ordering of costs, with no magnitudes. The thresholds are a design choice and must be disclosed as one.

**C5. Trust or Escalate** (arXiv 2407.18370 v1, 2024-07-25; University of Washington and AI2, now verified).
- The guarantee is P(agree | judge evaluates) ≥ 1-α with probability ≥ 1-δ. Experiments use δ = 0.1 and |Dcal| = 500.
- Under model-disjoint shift on ChatArena, over 1,000 splits, the guarantee success rate was 90.7-92.5% (100% at the 70% target). Coverage fell to 31.8% at the 90% target.
- That meets the nominal 90%. The dossier's "below the nominal level" is wrong.
- This is one shift type on one dataset.

**C6. Cresswell et al.** (ICML 2024; Layer 6 AI and TD Securities, industry).
- N = 50 per arm: 450 participants in the main study plus 150 in ablations, recruited on Prolific.
- Pre-registered, but the analysis moved from ANOVA to Welch t-tests.
- Participants were paid a bonus per correct answer. The dossier's "no money at stake" should read "no own money at risk".
- The accuracy p and d values for conformal vs top-k are verified. Most response-time comparisons were non-significant.

**C7. Arm B (novelty score).**
- A novelty threshold calibrated on held-out customers controls only the step_up rate on reference purchases that are assumed normal. It gives no bound on false approvals and no detection rate, because nothing in history labels "should have escalated".
- Historical approvals are issuer outcomes and the anomaly bursts are unlabelled. Neither is a fraud label or a wallet-policy label.
- Exchangeability between the 500 held-out customers and the scenario sessions is not established.
- Calibrating any score, extractor confidence included, estimates correctness only for the adjudicated target it was checked against. A score calibrated on `status` estimates issuer approval, not policy compliance.

**C8. H1 vs arm C (monotonicity).** An extractor that turns `item_details` into size or return-window facts can move a clause from unknown to satisfied, which enables approval. The claim that learned components only move decisions toward step_up or decline is therefore false for arm C.
- The defensible claim is narrower: merchant text supplies typed facts and never changes the policy, the thresholds or the decision function.
- Merchant-asserted facts can be false. No parser, regex or model detects a false claim that reads normally.
- Every approval that depended on a merchant-text fact should be flagged in the evidence.

**C9. G3 blocks legitimate cases.** A size of 42 in `item_details` should change the decision. Replace G3 with a paired test: inserting or removing instruction-like text while holding the typed facts fixed must leave the decision unchanged.

**C10. G5 (model-off and latency).**
- "Model-off identical or more conservative" is only a consistency check. All-step_up passes it, so it says nothing about correctness.
- It also breaks under `uncertainty_policy = approve`, because model-dependent unknowns would then be approved.
- The deadline is per request (`data.deadline_at`, counted from queueing). Report deadline misses with a target of zero, not p95. Measure the margin in the live run; public endpoint probes don't show venue latency.

**C11. H2 (step_up budget).** A budget must never turn an escalation into an approval. Deduplicate prompts for near-identical pending requests instead.

**C12. Statistics.**
- The 45 curated fixtures, and 300 or more rewrites of them, are correlated. They support per-invariant pass/fail counts only. The 299-trial zero-failure bound, 1-0.05^(1/n), does not apply.
- G4's binomial interval ignores clustering within customers. Use customer-level resampling or per-customer reporting.
- Compare arms on paired items. Overlapping marginal intervals do not show that there is no paired difference.
- The dossier's McNemar thresholds are correct two-sided exact values: 10:9, 20:15, 30:21, 40:27. With five or fewer discordant pairs, no two-sided p < 0.05 is possible.
- If a classifier scores better, that shows the rules miss information. It does not refute a rule invariant.

**C13. Test set (iii) is circular** if "ordinary" means historically approved, or if the same code writes the synthetic policy and its labels. Define "ordinary" as compliant with the synthetic policy, labelled by a second person or an independent script.

## Human factors (verified primary sources)
- **Ancker et al.** 2017, BMC Med Inform Decis Mak 17:36 (independent academic). Retrospective cohort: 112 clinicians, 1,266,325 reminders, 326,203 drug alerts.
  - Reminder acceptance IRR was 0.70 per extra reminder per encounter, and 0.90 per 5-point rise in the share of repeated reminders.
  - When a repeated reminder was overridden the first time, 87.9% of later instances were also overridden.
  - The design is observational at clinician level. The desensitization analysis rests on only 6 alerts and 46 clinicians.
- **Bondi et al.**, AAAI 36(5), 2022 (Harvard, DeepMind, Google Brain; abstract only). On conservation data, telling the human the system had deferred, without showing the AI's prediction, significantly improved human accuracy.

## Practical protocol
This is about 8 person-hours, can run in parallel across 2-3 people, and fits before the Sep 25 12:00 submission. The live steps need the event-day API key.

1. **Freeze interpretations (30 min).** For each clause, write the operational test, the fact source (platform field, history, run state or merchant text), how boundaries are handled, and the rule for unknowns.
2. **Blind adjudication (about 1.5 h each, two people).** For each of the 45 attempts:
   - clause verdicts {satisfied, violated, unknown}, each with its source;
   - an acceptable-action set A ⊆ {approve, step_up, decline} and a preferred action;
   - approve belongs to A only if both labellers include it.
   - Report raw agreement as well as kappa, because kappa is unstable on 45 items.
3. **Metamorphic relations (2 h).** Labels come from how each variant is constructed:
   - amount pushed over a cap: not approve;
   - an in-cap change to a compliant seed: decision unchanged;
   - an injection span added with facts unchanged: decision unchanged;
   - the same live ID delivered again: same result, spend counted once;
   - a near-duplicate order with a new ID: not approve;
   - null or unknown terms under `ask`: not approve;
   - boundary equality: as frozen in step 1.
   - Report violations per relation and the number of distinct seeds.
4. **Metrics.** Run the candidate in one harness alongside the all-approve, all-step_up, all-decline and rules-only baselines.
   - **U:** approvals where approve ∉ A. This is the primary metric; target zero and report the count.
   - **F:** non-approve decisions on items where A = {approve}. All-step_up scores F equal to the number of such items, so it cannot win once F is reported. If there are fewer than about 10 approve-only items, add compliant metamorphic variants and say so.
   - **Also report:** other mismatches, the preferred-action match rate, deadline misses, and the share of step_up cards that name both the clause and the fact.
5. **Model-off run.** Report every decision that changes and the rise in F. U must not rise.
6. **Live run.** All five scenarios, with each submission time compared against `deadline_at`, plus the re-delivery, resolve and revoke paths.
7. **Red team.** 45 minutes, timeboxed, by a teammate with code access. Report attempts and successes.
8. **Keep a model component** only if U and the metamorphic safety violations do not increase and F falls. Inspect every discordant item and report the exact McNemar test descriptively.

## Unresolved meanings and gaps
- What "used regularly" or "bought from before" means: how many purchases, over what window, and whether it is keyed on merchant_id or would also match lookalike sellers.
- What counts as an "ordinary grocery" item.
- How to recognise a "specialist sports retailer": category, MCC or text.
- Inclusive boundaries: exactly at the CHF cap, "14 days or more", and the edges of the seven-day window in simulated time.
- Whether a clear violation under "ask me when uncertain" should be declined or stepped up.
- Whether terms asserted by the merchant are enough to approve.
- Whether pending step_ups should reserve budget.
- Which signals mean "someone else is driving", and at what thresholds.
- Which monitor was "chosen": that fact is not in the event.
- How to tell a legitimate re-quote from a duplicate.
- What revocation does to queued requests, and what the 120 s timeout resolves to. The platform leaves both unspecified.
- How `uncertainty_policy = approve` should behave during a model outage.
- What the judging-round scenarios contain.

## Recommendation hypotheses (untested)
- **R1.** A clause engine over typed facts, with an explicit unknown state mapped through `uncertainty_policy`. Models act only as fact extractors that can abstain.
- **R2.** An evidence panel that shows each fact's source and flags merchant-asserted facts that enabled an approval.
- **R3.** The step_up card leads with the specific clause and the question, and deduplicates near-identical prompts.
- **R4.** In the pitch, claim counted invariant results and friction against the all-step_up baseline. Make no statistical, security or win guarantee. Conformal selective control is a production path once labelled target-distribution data exists.
