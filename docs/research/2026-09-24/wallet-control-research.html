Research conclusion

Build two connected parts: customer-confirmed permissions and a stateful purchase classifier. Jev is a credible candidate for classification. The research does not identify a measured winner for this wallet task.

Policy definition

Use a generative model to draft explicit permissions and questions about missing details. Show the customer the interpretation and a few boundary examples before activation. Keep the confirmed representation fixed while classifying purchases.

Purchase classification

Compare a deterministic baseline, protected direct Jev decisions and decomposed semantic questions. All candidates use the same budget calculations and state. Models may resolve uncertain product facts, with their errors measured explicitly.

The product angle I recommend testing is customer-controlled delegation of shopping. A short policy review shows what the agent is allowed to buy. The purchase view shows what happened under that permission and where the customer needs to decide. An adversarial purchase can make the need for this control visible in the one-minute pitch.

Training on the large history pack is optional. Its historical outcomes can support a behavior experiment, but they do not provide labels for the customer's policy or the “ask” decision. Give that experiment time only after the complete control flow works.

I checked the supplied data and reviewed published research. I have not run a model comparison on these purchases or a live simulator trial. The performance figures in this report come from the cited studies.

Part I: Policy definition

The input is the customer's instruction. The output is a confirmed, versioned representation of what the agent may purchase, together with explicit treatment of uncertain cases. This stage can ask questions and use a stronger generative model before a transaction is queued.

The difficult cases are often omissions: whether a mandate permits one purchase or repeated purchases, which history counts as “used before,” and what evidence establishes a specialist retailer. A model should surface those interpretations for review. It must not invent extra restrictions from a customer persona or silently remove a restriction it cannot represent.

Reviewed policy synthesis: AutoCedar, July 2026

AutoCedar separates interpretation from policy generation. A reviewer approves explicit statements about permitted behavior. The system then freezes that target and uses a verifier to guide code repairs. Its authors report success on all 221 CedarBench tasks with two model configurations. In a fixed 100-case ablation, schema-only generation passed 85 cases, a native verifier loop passed 94, and the complete feedback system passed 100. All received a reviewed schema, so this is not a comparison of raw natural-language prompts with no assistance. [AutoCedar]

Verification establishes conformity to the approved formal target. A reviewer can approve the wrong interpretation. The external evaluation covers three author-constructed scenarios. The 14-person human study compared policy artifacts and did not observe a live customer workflow.

The reported average synthesis times are 24.59 seconds for GPT-5.5 low and 8.93 seconds for Haiku 4.5. This work belongs before activation of the mandate. It cannot be repeated as the runtime authorizer under an eight-second queue-to-answer deadline.

A feasible adaptation is to show the customer each interpreted restriction and a few boundary examples before confirmation. For example, show how “used before” will be measured and whether a shoe-replacement mandate ends after one successful purchase. Validate the resulting rule representation. Boundary tests are useful evidence, but they do not constitute formal verification of the whole wallet.

Runtime governance: SAFR, July 2026

The industry-developed SAFR white paper, published through MAS, places a control checkpoint between an agent's proposal and execution. It separates identity, a controls repository, a disposition engine and an independent audit log. Controls can include both deterministic limits and semantic or probabilistic assessments. [SAFR]

SAFR also identifies a failure that matters here: an agent's proposed action and its explanation can be fabricated together and still agree with one another. Self-consistency is insufficient evidence. The proposal must be checked against independently established authority and observations.

SAFR is a reference framework with industry examples. It explicitly says it is not regulatory guidance. It supplies no measured wallet-classification advantage. Its useful contribution to this build is the separation of permissions, evidence, disposition and audit, plus explicit requirements for human-review capacity and deadlines.

Delegated-payment standards: AP2 and Verifiable Intent

The current AP2 v0.2 specification uses open and closed Checkout and Payment Mandates. Verification binds payment to the same checkout and requires evaluating its constraints. Older explanations using only “Intent Mandate” and “Cart Mandate” should not be copied into a v0.2 compatibility claim. [AP2]

Verifiable Intent's security specification states that transaction-count and cumulative-budget enforcement require stateful tracking. A fresh signed transaction can still consume the same mandate repeatedly. Nonces alone cannot enforce a total budget. The draft also documents the absence of a credential-layer revocation protocol in v0.1. [Verifiable Intent]

These sources support binding approvals to an exact purchase and tracking state across transactions. Their signatures do not establish that a seller's product description is truthful. Full AP2 or card-network integration is unnecessary for this sandbox; describe any borrowed design pattern without claiming protocol conformance.

Turn the instruction into a testable meaning

For each permission, store the original clause, its operational meaning, the facts needed to check it, and what happens when those facts are missing. Show assumptions to the customer before confirmation. For example, “used before” could refer to approved purchases on this card; “one replacement” could stop authorizing purchases after the first accepted order.

Review the whole policy as well as each individual rule. Test that adding a restriction cannot expand the allowed set for the same facts and state. Include examples that should remain allowed, so a compiler cannot appear correct by rejecting everything. Keep unsupported clauses visible instead of dropping them.

Part II: Purchase classification

The inputs are the confirmed policy, the proposed purchase and relevant historical and current state. The output is approve, decline or step_up, with the facts and rules supporting it. This is a state-dependent classification task. A pretrained model can contribute directly or answer smaller questions that are combined into the final action.

Jev belongs in the comparison

Jev can propose the three actions directly through a Choice. It can also answer separate questions about product compatibility or missing terms. No task-specific training labels are required to make those zero-shot calls. We still need labels to evaluate the answers and choose thresholds.

The strongest new independent evidence found in the review is a September 21 preprint on coding police crash narratives. The authors coded 195,857 narratives and audited the probabilities against 2,416 blinded human judgments. Jev achieved F1 0.908; Fable 5.1 achieved 0.967; GPT-5.6 Sol achieved 0.885, with its paired difference from Jev statistically inconclusive. The authors report a 0.20-second median and 0.33-second p90 for Jev in their setup. They also found task-specific miscalibration and improved it with labeled recalibration. [Crash-narrative study]

This is a first-version preprint about a different task, published three days before the review. It gives Jev a stronger basis for testing than launch marketing alone. It does not establish superiority over language models or calibration on wallet decisions.

Direct versus decomposed classification is still an experiment

TypeSafe recommends atomic questions and warns against hiding several judgments in one prompt. Its documentation also says Jev struggles with arithmetic, dates and counting, and that adversarial input can change the answer. Those are reasons to compute exact quantities in code and test decomposition. The review found no controlled independent comparison establishing that decomposition outperforms a direct three-way Jev question on this task. [Jev limitations]

Decomposition may make a mistake easier to locate: the system can identify whether size, return terms or product purpose caused the result. It also creates more opportunities for inconsistent answers. Direct classification may be faster to implement. Test both with the same information and the same hard authorization checks.

Decide what each model answer is allowed to do

A model used only as an extra veto can add a question or rejection. It cannot reduce the questions produced by the baseline. A model used to resolve an unknown fact can reduce friction, but a wrong answer can then enable a wrong approval. These are different roles, and they need separate measurements.

The enforceable restriction is that neither role can edit the confirmed policy or bypass a known hard violation. Semantic facts remain fallible. Sending the same text to two model prompts does not make their errors independent.

Confidence and latency need local measurements

Jev's Noul is a probability of “yes.” Choice and Score also return a derived confidence value. The general formula is not documented; a formula used in a demo is an approximation. Preserve the original probabilities and confidence in the log. If the team computes a margin or recalibrated score, name it separately. Do not interpret confidence 0.85 as an 85% chance that a purchase is authorized. [Confidence]

Published timing results vary. The crash study reports a 0.20-second median; FlowX reports p50 0.77 seconds and p95 1.13 seconds. A small independent gateway test reported a 29.5-second p95 over 100 calls. The client locations, routes and test setups differ. None measures this service at the venue. [Gateway experiment]

Use a request deadline, bounded retries and a tested fallback. A network probe without inference supplies no model-latency evidence. Pin the model and provider route used in the experiment. The vendor lists a price of $0.042 per million input tokens with free output, but price does not settle correctness or deadline reliability. [API and models]

For policy authoring, a generative model can draft the structured permissions and clarification questions. For runtime classification, Jev, a structured-output language model and a local model such as GLiNER2 are credible candidates. SetFit becomes relevant if the team collects enough labels to test a small specialized classifier. [SetFit]

What to do with the large history pack

A learned behavior score is a separate experiment. It can help identify unusual sessions, but training it on status gives an issuer-outcome predictor. It does not teach the model whether a shoe matches a customer instruction.

Recent tabular research gives a more complicated result than a blanket preference for tree models or foundation models. BeyondArena includes temporal and grouped splits that older IID-focused leaderboards omitted. The September 14 TabPFN-3.5 report claims first place overall on BeyondArena, while stating that tuned and ensembled MLPs still lead on its grouped, temporal and large-data subsets. These are benchmark results, with some protocol differences and wide intervals on small subsets. [BeyondArena] [TabPFN-3.5]

If the behavior experiment is worth doing, compare logistic regression with a modest LightGBM or CatBoost model. An explainable boosting machine is another option when inspecting individual feature effects matters. Include a foundation-model challenger if setup and licensing allow it. The TabPFN-3.5 code and weights have different licences; the report restricts commercial and production use of the weights and outputs. Check the intended use before choosing it for the entry or a later deployment.

The split and features need as much attention as the model. The data review found 8,569 rows on 67 cards where the card was active at transaction time but inactive in the reference snapshot. Using today's card status as a historical feature would leak future information. Merchant novelty also falls as the file accumulates history: purchases with no prior approved same-card merchant fall from 57.3% in September 2025 to 6.2% in July 2026. Allow a history warm-up and compare forward-time, customer-held-out results.

Graph and sequence models have published successes in other transaction settings. This pack supplies neither wallet-policy labels nor labeled session takeovers, and many useful history features are already computable. Their extra complexity would need a demonstrated benefit here before it displaced the policy UI or a working simulator integration.

State which evidence is trusted, and for what

  • The customer's confirmed policy defines permission. Merchant text must not change that policy, its thresholds or the code interpreting it.
  • The platform assigns mandate identity and live IDs and computes run context. The published fixtures supply purchase amounts and catalogue facts. The documentation describes their origin; this research has not observed a live event or authenticated real merchant data.
  • Merchant descriptions supply product claims. A size or return term can be interpreted correctly and still be false in the real world. Display the source and retain any conflict with structured facts.
  • The wallet's own record supplies prior accepted decisions. Bind an explanation and any human answer to the exact purchase and policy snapshot.

The challenge expects the service to use product facts from merchant text. In the shoe scenario, ten of twelve attempts have order_returnable = "true", with the window stated only in text. Refusing to use all text-derived facts would make many otherwise ordinary purchases require a question. Decide during policy confirmation what reliance on seller-stated terms is acceptable.

A parser or model cannot establish the truth of a seller's claim from that text alone. Deterministic checks can enforce a CHF cap against the supplied amount. Semantic checks can still misread the product. Keep those limits distinct when describing the prototype.

Use the security patterns without rebuilding the research systems

Keep authority separate from merchant input, check the proposed purchase before submitting a decision, and retain evidence for every outcome. Test exact numeric restrictions with the model returning adversarial answers. Test product interpretation separately with adversarial descriptions.

A full CaMeL, DRIFT or SIEVE integration has no demonstrated advantage for this single-decision service under the hackathon deadline. AgentDyn also shows that stronger isolation can lose substantial utility on open-ended tasks; its setup even disabled user confirmation, unlike this challenge. Transfer the mechanisms that fit, then measure their effect here. [AgentDyn]

Runtime requirements independent of model choice

These follow from the supplied contract. They need their own tests because a classifier benchmark will not reveal state or transport bugs. [API contract]

  1. Start the long-poll worker before the run. The default deadline begins at queueing, so waiting to receive the event consumes the same eight-second budget. A 204 poll response has no body and does not mean the run is finished.
  2. Use the live authorization_id for submissions and duplicate delivery. Keep source_authorization_id only for fixture mapping. Different live IDs can still describe a duplicate order.
  3. Use decimal money or correctly implemented integer minor units. The total already includes delivery. Apply the supplied FX rate to the row's currency with the specified rounding; merchant country does not determine currency.
  4. Calculate spending windows from simulated transaction timestamps. Use the real clock for response deadlines. Check each applicable rolling restriction against the appropriate state.
  5. Count accepted final approvals. A pending customer question is not an approval. Re-check headroom when resolving it, and serialize conflicting updates. Any local reservation must be tracked separately from approved spend.
  6. Reconcile ambiguous submission timeouts against platform state before treating them as rejected or sending spending that could exceed remaining authority. A request can have been accepted even when the client did not receive the response.
  7. Persist executable interpretations. The live event omits guidance and open_questions. Do not place a critical restriction only in those fields. The contract permits custom derived field names, provided the engine defines their meaning and obeys the allowed rule-value types.
  8. Respect snapshot semantics. Tightening a stored mandate affects later runs. The current run keeps its snapshot. Revocation effects on already queued or human-pending work are unspecified; show only cancellation confirmed by the platform.
  9. Use a real customer answer for /resolve. Keep polling while the UI waits. A purchase-specific confirmation must not silently broaden future authority.
  10. Budget model time from the actual remaining deadline. Reserve time for validation, state changes and submission. Bound retries, handle rate limits, and make model-off behavior explicit. Test the complete queue-to-submission path.

State also changes the interpretation of apparently compliant purchases. A legitimate re-quote after a declined order differs from another order after a successful one-time purchase. Two close purchases are evidence to inspect; their timing alone does not prove that they are one split order.

A late approval can break a later window

Consider a constructed example with a CHF 300 seven-day cap. A CHF 100 request dated August 10 waits for the customer. Two CHF 110 purchases dated August 11 and 12 are approved meanwhile. When the customer approves the first request, a check that looks only backward from August 10 sees CHF 100. But the seven-day window ending August 12 now contains CHF 320.

I checked that arithmetic locally. It is a test case, not an observed simulator outcome. If the simulator advances while a question is pending, finalizing an earlier request must account for all affected windows. A separate reservation scheme is another design choice, with a cost in available headroom while the question remains unanswered.

Have two people review the policy, facts and relevant prior outcomes without seeing the model's answer. For each requirement, record satisfied, violated or unresolved, with its evidence. Predefine how the team trades an unnecessary question against an unnecessary decline. The instruction “ask me when uncertain” does not specify numerical loss weights. Keep known hard violations outside that statistical trade-off.

Use paired comparisons. Hundreds of related rewrites are not hundreds of independent samples. Resample at the seed, policy or customer level where the sample supports it; with only a few groups, show the individual outcomes and leave the uncertainty visible. Confidence and conformal methods

Selective prediction studies the trade-off between automatic decisions and abstention. Conformal methods can control defined errors under stated calibration assumptions. Recent work includes Selective Conformal Risk Control and SCOPE. These are useful candidates once the team has adjudicated target data. They do not provide the missing labels. [CRC] [SCRC] [SCOPE]

Ordinary conformal coverage does not directly bound the error rate among approved purchases. A 5% marginal miscoverage bound with only 25% of purchases approved can imply a conditional bound as loose as 20%. The chosen loss and selection event matter. Adaptive attacks and distribution shift also undermine ordinary exchangeability assumptions.

For this build, use the labeled comparison to choose and document a threshold. Describe formal risk control as future work unless its assumptions and target loss have actually been checked. A threshold on a novelty score calibrated with historical approvals cannot establish a false-approval bound for wallet policies.

A one-minute demonstration

Use one customer instruction and its confirmed rules. Let a normal purchase complete. Show an attempted instruction override and the independent rule it cannot change. Then show a genuinely unresolved purchase and a real human decision. End with the control log and revocation state. Keep the full replay, error cases and model comparison available for Q&A.

For the human question, display the amount, merchant, exact constraint and missing or conflicting fact. Distinguish the seller's claim from the wallet's own evidence. Avoid presenting the model's recommendation as the customer's default choice. Do not place persuasive injection text in an ordinary approval message.

Ancker's observational study of clinical reminders found lower acceptance as reminder burden increased. It provides a reason to test repeated questions here, with no basis for importing its effect size into payments. Count repeated prompts and check whether a person can understand the question without reading the raw event. [Alert fatigue]

Any claim in the pitch should match a recorded result: “these tested caps held when the model was disabled” is narrower and more defensible than “the AI is injection-proof.” Mention only measurements that have actually been run.

Questions for Viseca

Resolve these before assigning a single “correct” action to every public fixture. The documentation leaves room for different policies.

  • Does “a shop I have bought from before” mean prior approved purchases on this card, across the customer's cards, or another scope? May a purchase approved earlier in the run establish familiarity?
  • Do the shoe-replacement and chosen-monitor mandates authorize exactly one completed purchase? How should later compliant alternatives and re-quotes be interpreted after success?
  • Which event facts are independently supplied or verified by the simulator, and which represent a potentially compromised shopping agent or seller's claims?
  • What is the confirmed effect of revocation on queued requests and outstanding step_ups? What status follows human timeout?
  • When another approval consumes the budget during a pending question, how should the resolution be rejected or reconciled? Does the platform enforce any participant-defined spend rules itself?
  • How should the team demonstrate useful uncertainty handling where several actions are defensible? The public data intentionally provides no answer key.

Limits and corrections

Earlier advice in this conversation was too categorical about classifiers. Jev can propose a direct three-way decision. Zero-shot inference does not require a task-specific training set. Models still require suitable evaluation before they earn trust in this workflow.

  • Jev outputs numerical probabilities and scores. It does not generate arbitrary free-form values. “Cannot output numbers” was inaccurate.
  • A confidence cutoff of 0.85 was an illustrative guess. It was not calibrated for these policies. Confidence semantics differ across primitives and adapters.
  • A narrow question to a model can still affect an unsafe approval. An incorrectly inferred return window can satisfy a downstream rule. Typed output narrows the format; it does not establish the truth of a fact.
  • A regex is not a general solution to adversarial semantics. It can select the wrong number, misunderstand negation, or faithfully extract a false seller claim.
  • Historical decline associations do not establish fraud, identity compromise or causation. A population statistic should not be presented as a personalized probability for one purchase.
  • A report of zero failures on curated fixtures is evidence about those cases. Even with 45 genuinely independent representative trials, zero failures would give an exact one-sided 95% upper error bound of about 6.44%. These 45 fixtures do not satisfy that sampling premise. Hundreds of rewrites from the same few seeds do not fix it.
  • No published benchmark located in this review establishes a best classifier for these five policies, live-state branches and attack surfaces together. Literature can shortlist methods; the local comparison must select the implementation.

Research method

The search covered four topic lanes using Opus 5.5: semantic classification, injection defenses, selective prediction and human escalation, and behavioral models. Separate Opus 5.5 reviews checked the evidence. GPT-6 Sol then reviewed the combined conclusions and reopened primary sources. The workflow requests used xhigh effort.

Tavily and TinyFish supplied discovery and source text, with direct reads where needed. Tavily reached its search quota. The first workflow stopped advancing between stages; its work was preserved and the review stage was run separately. The completed review workflow returned all five reports without an agent error.
