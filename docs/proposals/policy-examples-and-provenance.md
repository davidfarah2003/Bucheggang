## 1. Executable boundary examples

AutoCedar's reviewed floors, ceilings and liveness slices are symbolic properties over an approved request universe (local paper `docs/papers/2607.03656v1.pdf`, section III-B, pages 3 to 4). Our proposed slice checks only *particular authored purchases*. It is a finite consistency check and gives no coverage or semantic-equivalence guarantee.

Current behavior: `src/leash/policy/store.py:170-178` validates the shape of agent-authored `examples[]` and accepts their claimed `expected` outcome. `src/leash/contracts/policy.py:68-71` has only description, expected and why. `app/app.js:463-478` shows those claims. Plan 01 step 3 calls for `evaluate` at draft time, and its Log explicitly says this is absent (`docs/plans/01-policy-confirmation.md:43-49,101`). The authoring guide currently asks for an allowed, forbidden and unknown example (`src/leash/policy/mcp_server.py:57-73`). A source phrase proves only textual attribution, not that an agent's rule captures the customer's intent.

**Interface for owner agreement:** retain the current display-only `examples[]` for existing drafts. Add optional `boundary_cases[]` to `PolicyDraft`; legacy drafts omit the field and retain their exact v1 hash behavior. Each case contains `description`, `expected`, a *complete* strict `Event`, per-line `PurchaseFacts`, a complete `MandateState`, an explicit frozen `History` slice, and `why`. The engine's `evaluate` needs an optional explicit history input, with packaged `HISTORY` as the default for existing callers; the exact serializable history shape is an engine-owned contracts decision. No implicit case-input defaults, synthetic customer responses, scenario-specific branches or live simulator I/O. A case identifies the exact cart, amount, merchant, timestamp, policy/mandate snapshot and accepted prior approvals; `Event.mandate` reproduces the effective confirmed rules and uncertainty policy for that case. Require `Event.authorization.mandate_id == Event.mandate.mandate_id == state.mandate_id`, matching authorization and item IDs, policy instruction and effective rule list. These are **synthetic authored inputs with locally computed outcomes**, not verified purchase intent or independently authenticated merchant evidence. Reject any unrepresentable requirement as an open question rather than presenting an example as verified.

At proposal creation and revision, validate each case against `evaluate(event, candidate_policy, state, facts, history=case.history)` from `src/leash/engine/evaluate.py:42-98`, after the engine has agreed and implemented that interface. Reject mismatched `expected`, malformed facts/provenance and inconsistent input identities. Re-evaluate at confirmation before any simulator call and persist the observed outcome, case-input hash, policy version and engine version on the confirmation. Introduce an explicit `hash_version`: v1 continues hashing only instruction, rules and uncertainty (`src/leash/policy/store.py:88-92`) for existing drafts; v2 covers all v1 fields plus the complete canonical `boundary_cases[]` and pinned evaluator version for new or revised drafts. Never migrate a stored v1 hash silently. Changes to any v2 case require a new draft version and exact-version/hash confirmation. `RuleContext.history` now defaults to packaged `HISTORY` (`src/leash/engine/rules.py:45-60`); a synthetic `Event` alone cannot freeze familiarity, country or lookalike checks (`src/leash/engine/checks.py:105-149,204-217`). Input completeness is necessary for a reproducible result, but an agent-authored boundary still needs human intent review.

**Minimal first demonstration after the hold:** author one synthetic case per expected action with explicit frozen history that supports familiar device, merchant and country on the approve case, plus known facts, one forbidden amount, and one missing explicitly required fact under `uncertainty_policy=ask`. Show computed outcomes and rejection of a deliberately mislabeled case without a simulator request. Empty history itself triggers `no_card_history`, so it cannot demonstrate a clean approval under `ask`. Existing `docs/samples/scen0002_draft.json` is illustrative, not an independent intent oracle. A one-off route/MCP proposal through a temporary store and a stale-version confirmation conflict are appropriate checks; no tests, fixtures for tests, suite or linter are added. App output separates computed case result, its authored input facts and policy version from the current agent-authored `draft.examples`, with an unmistakable synthetic label and no implication of a live purchase or customer answer.

## 2. Enforced source precedence on one conflicting claim

CaMeL isolates data from a trusted program's control flow (local `docs/papers/2503.18813v2.pdf`, sections 3 to 5). Fides uses integrity labels propagated across an agent loop and checks tools against them (`https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23643v2`, sections 4.1 to 4.4). Our proposed slice is narrower: deterministic source precedence over one typed cart-line fact. It is neither a CaMeL capability runtime nor Fides IFC/noninterference.

Current behavior: `src/leash/extract/facts.py:79-113` may choose a local catalogue's item name/category, parse merchant copy for size, return days and risk markers, and attach sources. `facts.py:120-151` currently turns unequal normalized names into `matches_request=false`. An instruction regex marks a line (`facts.py:20-29,115-118`); `src/leash/engine/evaluate.py:28-35` then erases all extracted facts for it, including any independently structured product name, while `engine/checks.py:220-226` reports uncertain. Existing `engine/checks.py:51-75` can hard-decline based on item-name mismatch or add-on markers. No part of this is a general IFC label propagation system. A matched regex is not evidence that an unrecognized injection is harmless.

**Interface for owner agreement:** keep existing three-valued facts and never infer a negative from silence. Extract owner proposes a closed enum or explicit per-line conflict fields in `PurchaseFacts` for contradictory observed merchant terms (`"No returns. Returns accepted within 30 days."`, two sizes) and structured catalogue versus event name/category differences. Current conflicting terms already collapse to unknown but only carry `sources.<field>=merchant_text`; they need a distinct marker. Engine owner agrees how a marked low-trust conflict produces an `uncertain` check, never `pass`, while independently sourced structured values remain intact. Catalogue-backed `sources.product_type=structured` applies only when the item ID resolves; uncatalogued item names stay `merchant_text`. The current live runner calls `extract_event` without a catalogue (`src/leash/runner/loop.py:145-155`), so no live `product_type` has catalogue-backed `structured` provenance now: this slice cannot claim improved live item identity without a separate runner/data-source contract. Simulator fields are structured, but production merchant signatures are unavailable. An unrecognized synonym between an instruction and item name is not proof of fraud or a catalogue contradiction; surface it as unknown/open question when required.

The narrow first engine slice changes `_quarantine` so a recognized injected line drops only `merchant_text` and `agent_form` facts and retains independently checked `structured` fields, while the injected-instructions check remains uncertain (`src/leash/engine/evaluate.py:28-35`; `src/leash/engine/checks.py:220-226`). Marked contradictions also remain uncertain and never upgrade low-trust text to a trusted source. The changed fact/check contract needs engine and extract agreement in a separate PR before either lane edits. A further source-conflict reason code also needs `src/leash/contracts/decision.py` and `docs/contracts.md`. Under `uncertainty_policy=approve` the existing engine maps uncertain-only to approve (`src/leash/engine/evaluate.py:76-84`), so this slice cannot promise unconditional blocking of injected text. Do not remove explicit hard-rule failures on amount, merchant or status. Changing provenance can change evidence or outcomes; compare all 45 offline rows before review.

**Minimal one-off evidence after the hold:** compare a clean known catalogue line, an injected line whose catalogue identity remains structured while text-only return period becomes unknown, a contradictory return/size line carrying its explicit conflict marker, and an uncatalogued line whose merchant-supplied name remains untrusted. Inspect output from one local `extract_item`/`evaluate` invocation and the existing offline replay once after edits; list each changed row. With `ask` and no other fail, injection must lead to `step_up`; with `approve` it may not. The regex currently misflags `"not a subscription, one-off payment"` because it sees `subscription`; negation handling is a separate extract-only follow-on requiring engine-owner approval before behavior changes. A conflicting merchant phrase that the parser cannot recognize stays unproven. No claim of general injection resistance, accuracy beyond the authored corpus, or model use. No simulator, test suite or linter.

**Touch/ownership:** extract owner owns `src/leash/extract/facts.py` and the existing `docs/samples/extract-corpus.jsonl`; engine owner owns `src/leash/engine/evaluate.py` and `checks.py`, waiting for #47 to settle; engine also owns `src/leash/contracts/decision.py` and `src/leash/contracts/policy.py` under a separate agreed contract PR.
